HackedSelf

I write evidence-first biohacking reviews, where a protocol gets read the way a receipt gets read: what is the dose, what does it cost, what would change the answer. Home heat is the cleanest example, because the numbers are small enough to check by hand.

Per session, not per month

A two-person electric sauna pulls about 6 kilowatts, and a real session is 45 minutes of preheat plus a half hour inside, so about 7.5 kilowatt-hours on the meter. At 18 cents a kilowatt-hour that is $1.35 a session. Recovery lounges charge by the visit, commonly $40 with a day pass. The gap is $38.65 per session, every session.

Where break-even lands

A decent home unit runs about $2,800 installed. Divided by the $38.65 gap, break-even sits at 72 sessions. At three sessions a week that is about five and a half months, and the year-one math gets blunt: 150 sessions at home cost $202 in power on top of the unit, against $6,000 at the lounge. The device pays for itself inside the first year, and the open question is whether the sessions actually happen, which is a scheduling problem, not a money one.

James Verlander, Poket Dev LLC, Detroit
