I write evidence-first biohacking reviews, where a protocol gets read the way a receipt gets read: what is the dose, what does it cost, what would change the answer. Sleep debt is the cleanest example, because the ledger is small enough to keep by hand.

The ledger rule

Sleep need sits at a stable personal baseline; for most adults it is between seven and nine hours a night. The ledger logs the gap below that baseline, not the total hours. A night of six hours against an eight-hour need books two hours of debt. A night of nine books zero, because the ledger does not credit surplus against future shortfalls. Weekend recovery sleep feels real but does not erase the arithmetic; the point of keeping the ledger is to see the week as it is instead of averaging it away.

Week total: 5.5 hours of debt across five short nights. The two weekend nights above baseline change nothing on the ledger, and that is the feature: the number says what the week cost, not what it averaged.

What the balance is for

The ledger earns its keep when the same week repeats. Four weeks at 5.5 hours is three lost nights of sleep, sitting on the books at the same time every month. That is the pattern worth acting on, and no morning rating catches it. Reaction time and mood shift night to night for a dozen reasons; the cumulative gap moves on a schedule, and a ledger is the cheapest instrument that reads it. When the balance climbs, the fix that holds is usually structural, an earlier lights-out or a shorter evening commute, rather than a longer Saturday.
